Common Health Insurance Terms You Should Know

Understanding the terms and conditions of your health insurance is crucial for policyholders to avoid last-minute hassles. Read out these commonly used health insurance terms to make reading the policy documents easier:

  • Co-payment: Some insurance policies have an inbuilt copayment clause. Copayment, or ‘Copay’, is a specific amount or percentage an insured individual must pay from his/her pocket every time a claim is raised. Simply put, it is a cost-sharing concept between the policyholder and the insurance company.
  • Deductible: A deductible is the amount you pay from your pocket when a claim is raised. Here is an example of how deductibles in health insurance work: If your health insurance claim amount is Rs. 2,00,000 and your policy’s deductible amount is ₹50,000, then you pay ₹50,000. At the same time, the health insurance company pays the balance of ₹1,50,000.
    • So, it is essential to check your medical insurance policy documents for a deductible clause and choose one without a deductible unless you are okay with bearing a part of the treatment cost.
  • Sum insured: When you buy a health insurance policy, you must choose a coverage amount up to which your health insurance company will cover the medical expenses. The higher the sum insured, the higher the premium.
  • Top-up plans: These are the best health insurance plans that help you get more coverage if your basic sum insured is exhausted during a medical emergency. Top-up or super top-up policies can be purchased along with a base policy.
  • Pre-existing Diseases: If someone suffers from an illness before purchasing a health plan, it is called a pre-existing disease. Some common examples are diabetes, asthma, hypertension, or high BMI. People with a pre-existing illness can also be eligible for best health insurance policy in india. However, they generally have to pay a slightly higher premium or serve a pre-decided waiting period to be able to file a claim against the condition.
  • Claim Intimation: Claim intimation in health insurance refers to informing insurance providers about the upcoming claim. This usually involves notifying the insurance provider about hospitalisation medical treatment before submitting detailed claim information. It enables insurers to be aware beforehand to streamline the subsequent claims processing. It is important to know that in some cases if the insured person does not submit an insurance claim intimation, the claim might be rejected.
  • Cashless Hospitalisation Claim: A cashless hospitalisation claim is a facility offered by medical insurance providers to help policyholders access medical services without worrying about the availability of upfront finances. To use this facility best, one has to receive treatment at network hospitals affiliated with the insurance provider.
  • Reimbursement Claim: The term reimbursement claim refers to the repayment of the claim. In this type of claim settlement, you pay the medical/hospitalisation bills for the medical treatment you avail of. Once you submit the bills to your health insurance provider, you can file a claim and get the covered amount from the insurer.
  • No Claim Bonus: No claim bonus is an additional benefit offered under a health insurance plan to get a complimentary bonus for every claim-free year. It helps boost the coverage of your health insurance plan.
  • Sublimits: A sub-limit is a predetermined capping applied by the insurer on certain medical expenses under your private health insurance. It works as a limit on how much claim amount the insurer will pay for a given healthcare expense. Sub-limits are usually calculated in percentage and apply to both cashless and reimbursement claims.
  • Room Type: Room type refers to the kind of hospital room a policy covers. Common categories include general ward, semi-private room, and private room. The type of room you choose can directly impact the overall cost of your hospitalisation bill.
  • Room Rent Proportionate Charge: Room Rent Proportionate Charge limits the amount an insurer will pay for room rent per day during hospitalisation. If you exceed the room rent limit, you must pay the difference out of your pocket.
  • Smart Select Hospitals: Smart select at Care Health Insurance is an add-on benefit facility that allows an insured to pay 15% lesser premium and get coverage up to sum insured at Care’s Smart Select Network Hospitals. Please note that getting treatment out of the smart select network may borne a 20% copayment or as specified in the policy documents.
  • Use of Preventive Care: Taking timely precautions is the need of the hour.Under preventive care, best mediclaim policy in india that cover COVID treatment, annual health check-ups, general OPD visits, diagnosis, medical tests, consultations, etc., done to analyse any severe health patterns beforehand. By paying the premium, you can avail of preventive care benefits under your affordable medical insurance plans.
  • Alternate Treatments in Health Insurance: Alternative medicines are better known as AYUSH treatments, which stand for Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy. These treatments are deemed safe and effective not just in India but worldwide.

Some Myths about Health Insurance Busted

Health insurance is vital to financial planning, yet various misconceptions surround it. Let us examine the most common health insurance myths and help you understand what is a myth and what is not.

  • Health Insurance is only meant for seniors: Health insurance is crucial for people of all ages, owing to the increase in health risks in younger individuals. Further, early purchases also lead to lower premiums.
  • Coverage Begins Immediately Upon Purchase: Usually, a waiting period of 30 days applies before you can make a claim. Specific conditions may also have additional waiting periods.
  • The Cheapest Plan is the Best Choice: The cheapest plan may not offer comprehensive coverage. To be properly covered, choose adequate benefits over price.
  • Pre-Existing Diseases Disqualify You From Health Insurance: Pre-existing diseases often result in higher waiting periods or premiums, but you can still get coverage. Specific add-ons are also available to reduce waiting periods.
  • Group Health Plan is Enough: Group plans usually have limited coverage and can be ineffective in case of job switch. Consider purchasing an individual plan to supplement existing group coverage.
  • All Hospital Bills are Covered: Even the most comprehensive health insurance plans have specific exclusions. Read your policy documents to understand policy limitations.

Q. Should You Get a Health Insurance Policy at an Early Age?

Health insurance protects your savings by covering medical expenses. Care Health Insurance offers various benefits, including cashless claims, tax savings, and coverage for hospitalisation, critical illnesses, and more.

Q. Why is Health Insurance Policy Important to Protect Your Loved Ones?

Health insurance is important to protect your loved ones as it offers financial security for medical costs, ensuring access to care and peace of mind.

Q. Does Health Insurance Cover COVID-19 Expenses?

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India has stated that companies should offer coverage for standard treatment of COVID-19 infections if the insured is affected by the infection and has to get hospitalised for the treatment.

Q. How much health policy insurance coverage do I need?

The amount of insurance coverage you will need depends on your medical needs, age and health risks, the city you live in, and various other factors. Also, you must consider the treatment costs for your specific health condition before deciding how much sum insured is enough for you and your loved ones.

Q. What documents do I need to purchase mediclaim health insurance?

While purchasing a mediclaim insurance policy, you will require a few important documents to complete the process. Some of the documents required include ID proof, age proof, income proof documents, age proof, photographs, and relevant medical records.

Q. Can I buy health insurance and use it immediately?

Yes, you can get immediate coverage under medical policies for any accidents or injuries. However, you can only avail of the policy's coverage for any illness- related hospitalisation after a standard waiting period of 30 days from the day your policy becomes effective.

Q. What is the right age to invest in Health Insurance? Which is the best health insurance for myself and my family?

The earlier you get health cover, the lesser the premium you get to bear. The premium costs of health plans increase with age. Besides, the chances of having a pre-existing health condition also increase as we grow older- resulting in an increased premium. Thus, going for a health cover early in life is a wise decision.

Q. What is the benefit of porting health insurance?

Medical insurance portability can help you switch a health insurance plan without having to go through a new waiting period. Once you have finished the waiting period in your previous plan, you do not have to serve it again.

Q. How much does health insurance cost?

At Care Health Insurance, a 5-lakh medical cover starts with a premium as low as Rs. 358/month, subject to specific policy terms and conditions. Factors like the sum insured, type of coverage, policy term, count and age of insured members, and their pre-existing medical conditions, impact the chosen health plan's premium cost.

Q. How can I get tax benefits from medical covers?

With our medical insurance, you get a chance to increase your savings as you become eligible to get income tax benefits under Section 80D of the Income Tax Act. That is, you will be allowed to claim a tax deduction on the policy premium for which you have made a payment to us.

Q. Can I have multiple health insurance plans in India?

Yes, you can buy yourself multiple health policies. If you have opted for more than one indemnity-based plan with other insurers or us, then you can opt for any of these plans to settle your claims, provided that the claim amount payable is up to the sum insured of the chosen policy. If the claim amount under a single policy exceeds the sum insured, you have the right to select the insurer to settle the claim.

Q. Can I add my ageing parents to my medical insurance policy?

Yes, you can include ageing parents in your health insurance plan easily by informing us during renewal. However, for elderly parents above 60 years, we recommend medical insurance for senior citizens that will better meet their age-related healthcare needs.

Q. How does smoking affect the insurance premium for medical cover?

Insurers consider the habit of smoking as a factor when assessing your hea health risks and calculating insurance premium rates. Generally, smokers need to bear a higher premium for any health cover, given that smoking makes people more vulnerable to chronic diseases and other health hazards.

Q. I'm covered under a corporate group policy. Do I still need a separate health cover?

Yes, it is wiser always to have separate healthcare insurance besides the employer health plan because the corporate plan does not offer extensive coverage, no tax benefits, and customisable features. Thus, it is better to protect your finances and health with a separate cover that meets all your healthcare needs effectively.

Q. Can I take a health insurance policy for my 5 years old kid?

Yes, you can include your children aged 5 years or above under an individual health plan. For children under 5 years of age, you can include them in a family floater plan with other family members who are 18 years and above.

Q. Are medical tests mandatory while buying health and medical insurance?

Pre-policy medical tests are usually required for older individuals, those with a pre-existing condition, or opted for a higher sum insured. However, it is best to go for a medical check-up when opting for a health insurance plan to ensure hassle-free claim settlement.

Q. How can I increase the sum insured for my health insurance?

You can increase the sum insured of your health plan at the time of policy renewal. The same can be done after specific medical tests and a revision of the claim history. But the sum insured cannot be increased further if the existing sum has already met the sum insured limit of your policy.

Q. What is the waiting period for health insurance?

The waiting period is the time you must wait before making a claim for specific conditions, as stated in your policy documents.

Q. What is copayment in health insurance?

Co-payment can be defined as a cost-sharing feature in which the insured must pay a fixed amount of the costs incurred while filing a claim; the insurer will cover the rest.

Q. What is a deductible in health insurance?

A deductible is the amount the insured must pay out-of-pocket before the insurance cover comes into effect.

Q. What is a network hospital?

A network hospital is a hospital which has partnered with your insurance company, enabling you to seek cashless treatment.

Q. What is the sum insured in health insurance?

The Sum Insured is the maximum amount the insurer will cover for medical expenses. A higher sum insured means a higher premium.

Q. What are exclusions in health insurance?

Exclusions can be defined as certain conditions which are not covered by the health insurance policy.

Q. What are top-up plans in health insurance?

Top-up plans provide extra coverage over your base sum insured, ensuring additional protection during medical emergencies.

Q. What are pre-existing diseases in health insurance?

Pre-existing diseases are conditions that the insured has before purchasing a plan. They usually lead to higher premiums or waiting periods.

Q. What is claim intimation in health insurance?

Claim intimation is the process of informing your insurer about an upcoming claim, mostly before hospitalisation. This helps ease the claim process.

Q. What is a cashless hospitalisation claim?

Cashless claim settlement is when the insurer directly compensates the hospital for your treatment, bypassing the need for upfront payment.

Q. What is a reimbursement claim?

A reimbursement claim is one in which the insurer pays the medical costs upfront and then gets reimbursed by the insurance company.

Q. What is a No Claim Bonus in health insurance?

It is a bonus for policyholders who do not make any claims during a policy year; this often increases the coverage amount.

Q. What are Sublimits in health insurance?

Sublimits can be defined as the maximum limit of the amount the insurance company will pay for certain medical expenses.

Q. What is the Room Rent Proportionate Charge?

This is the maximum limit of the daily room cost covered by the insurance company; any extra amount is your responsibility.

Q. What is domiciliary hospitalisation?

Domiciliary hospitalisation is a situation in which the insured receives treatment at home either because the doctor has declared that they cannot be admitted to the hospital, or there is a non-availability of rooms at the hospital.

Q. What are network and non-network hospitals?

Network hospitals are empanelled with a health insurer and provide you with cashless medical treatment. Here, you can present your health card to avail yourself of a cashless hospitalisation facility, and the insurer will directly settle the claim with the hospital. Meanwhile, non-network hospitals are not empanelled with the insurer and thus require reimbursement claim filing.

Q. What are the pre-existing diseases in health insurance? Will my health policy cover the same?

Pre-existing diseases are illnesses, injuries, or related conditions for which a medical practitioner diagnosed a person within 36 months before the effective date of policy issuance by the insurer or its reinstatement. Yes, Care Health Insurance policies cover pre-existing medical conditions but come with a waiting period as per the chosen policy.

Q. What is the Smart Select Hospitals benefit at Care Health Insurance?

It is an additional cover option that reduces your premium by 15%, provided you use Care's Smart Select Network Hospitals, but using non-network hospitals may result in a 20% co-payment.

Q. How does Room Type affect my health insurance coverage?

The type of room you choose during your hospitalisation impacts your hospital bill. Policies usually have coverage limits for each room type.

Q. Is overseas treatment of an illness covered in my insurance plan?

Generally, any type of treatment taken overseas is not covered. However, you can opt for a global coverage option if you wish to continue treatment abroad.

Q. How does no claim bonus work in a healthcare insurance policy?

A no-Claim Bonus (NCB) that is given to the policyholder for not raising any claim during a policy year. It is a bonus amount that becomes available as an increased sum insured for every claim-free year. The NCB amount will not exceed 50% of the sum insured under any medical policy insurance. The accrued bonus is reduced by 10% in the next policy year, if the insurer claims in a policy year.

Q. Do health insurance policies cover robotic surgery & modern treatments?

Yes, our specialised health insurance plans cover various expenses under advanced medical treatments, including robotic surgery and modern-day care treatments. We offer cover for more than 541 daycare procedures

Q. Is there a grace period for renewal under health insurance?

Yes, an insured person is allowed a grace period of 30 days immediately following the policy period end date to pay the renewal premium for a Care insurance policy. You can pay the premium within the grace period to renew the benefits of your medical health insurance policy.

Q. Does health insurance get renewed automatically?

No, you might have to pay a premium and follow a renewal policy to get your health plan renewed.

Q. What happens if I cannot renew my insurance on time?

Health insurance renewal should be done before the policy expiry date or within the grace period to retain all the bonuses and and get continued coverage.

Q. Is it safe to renew health insurance online?

Yes, you can visit your insurer's official website to renew your health insurance plan from the comfort of your home.

Q. What is the process for a reimbursement claim settlement of medical insurance?

Typically, you will have to inform the insurance company about your hospitalisation. Submit the claim form and all the stamped and verified documents within the stipulated timeline. Once the claim is verified, it will be settled within 2-3 weeks.

Q. How many claims can I make in a medical insurance policy?

You can make multiple insurance claims during the policy term, provided that it remains within the sum insured limit.

Q. What are the details required to make a health insurance claim?

For a cashless claim, you need to submit the pre-authorization form at the hospital's TPA desk. Also, you need to present your health card and identity proof at the network hospital. Under a reimbursement claim, submit the claim form and documents, such as identity proofs and hospital bills. The details required include name, contact information, policy number, past medical history, proposed line of treatment, duration of treatment, etc.

Q. What is the process for a reimbursement claim settlement of medical insurance?

Typically you will have to inform the insurance company about your hospitalisation. Submit the claim form and all the stamped and verified documents within the stipulated timeline. Once the claim is verified, it will be settled within 2-3 weeks.
